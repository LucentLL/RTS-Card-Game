## Consequences of G1

* `GAPS.md` G1 is **closed**. Its proposed action (add an art backlog, gate M13's "done when" on
  coverage) is **rejected** — M13 ships with whatever art exists.
* The 3-tier art fallback (`spec/06`, from `02_art.js`) is therefore **load-bearing in the shipped
  build**, not just in development. It must be ported faithfully and must look deliberate rather
  than broken: a card with no `_cardart` still needs a readable frame, name, cost and stats.
* The importer must not error or warn-spam on a missing texture — a missing art file is the
  expected case, not an exception.
* Reopen only if the release scope changes (e.g. a paid Steam launch where placeholder art becomes
  a store-page problem). That is a business decision, not an engineering one.

## Session decisions — 2026-08-21 (M10)

| # | Question | Answer | Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D6** | The JS springs traps two different ways — the AI's auto-spring (`foeTrapOnSummon`, `springAttackTrap`) and the human's choice (`playerTrapOnSummon`, replaced at load time by `RESP.defendWindow`). Which one is canonical? | **Neither: the defender is always ASKED.** The core has no idea which side is a person, so an asymmetry keyed on "is this the AI" is not expressible in it. Both halves become a parked `ResponseWindowRequest` the defender answers with `TrapChosen`. A policy that answers "the first armed trap" reproduces the old auto-spring outcome exactly, and that is what the stand-in opponent does. The human gains the choice the RESP layer already gave them. | 2026-08-21 |
| **D7** | The JS opened ONE response window per *action* (`foeTurn` consumes `springRef` once) but auto-sprang per *spring site* when the AI held the trap. Which granularity does the port use? | **Per spring site.** The resolver offers the window at every point where `springAttackTrap` would have fired, and re-reads the armed list each time. This is the strict generalisation: answering "spring" at every site is bit-for-bit the auto path, and a defender holding two attack traps can still spring both in one resolution, as the JS auto path could. A defender holding none is never asked, so combat with no traps is unchanged. | 2026-08-21 |
| **D8** | `trigger:'summon'` traps ignore `card.effect` entirely in the JS — any of them just destroys the newcomer. Port the quirk or honour the effect? | **Port the quirk.** All three summon traps are Snare today so it is invisible, and the data model implying otherwise is not worth a divergence while the differential harness (M12) is still the gate. Pinned by a test that gives a summon trap a `burn` effect and asserts the creature is destroyed anyway. | 2026-08-21 |
| **D9** | `RulesHooks.OnCreatureEnter` / `OnSummonTrap` were assignable static delegates left as M10 seams. Keep the indirection? | **Deleted.** They are direct calls into `Triggers` / `KeywordEngine` now. A mutable static hook is a live hazard the moment two matches share a process (AI search clones state thousands of times a second), and with the keyword milestone landed there is nothing left to defer. `DeathSweep.OnCreatureDeath` went the same way. | 2026-08-21 |
| **D10** | A bounced or revived creature came back as its CATALOG card, losing hatched forms and Thornmail buffs (the M8 debt). | **Cards carry a `CreatureSnapshot`.** `HandCard`, `GraveRecord` and `ChargeUnit` each hold the live statline when the card has been on the board, mirroring the JS's `handcardFromCreature` / `toGrave` object literals field for field — including what they deliberately DROP (`cnt`, `oc`, `bank`, `token`). A card that never left the deck carries none and still resolves through the catalog. The flag is hashed, so a card with history cannot hash equal to a fresh copy. | 2026-08-21 |
| **D11** | PORT_PLAN M10 asked for `ISpellEffectHandler` × 6, mirroring the keyword registry. | **A switch on `SpellEffect` instead.** The keyword registry earns an interface because it has six hook points to implement; a spell has exactly one operation and one predicate, and two of the six effects (pitfall, thornmail) have no castable branch at all — six near-empty classes would be shape without substance. The requirement that actually mattered — dispatch on EFFECT, never on card name, and hold target legality in one place — is met: adding an effect touches `SpellTargeting.CanTarget` and `SpellEngine.Resolve`, nothing else. | 2026-08-21 |
| **D12** | The 14-agent audit of M10 confirmed that a Scour flier Undertow hurls back to hand *mid-resolution* still shatters a back-row card in the JS — it strikes from its owner's hand, because the JS resolver walks captured attacker OBJECTS while ours walks ids. Reproduce or fix? | **Reproduce.** It is the JS's own bug, and parity is the contract until the M12 differential harness is green. Implemented without breaking resumability: the bounce is recorded in `CombatState.BouncedScourIds` (serialized, cloned, hashed) rather than by holding a live object reference a snapshot would lose. Pinned by `AnUndertowBouncedFlier_StillShattersTheBackRow`. Improving it later means deleting the list, not unpicking a design. | 2026-08-21 |
| **D13** | The JS AI turn is one async function that mutates the board directly and awaits modals mid-combat. What shape does it take in the port? | **A command SOURCE, not a coroutine.** `ScriptedAiPolicy.Next(engine)` answers one question - "what would you do next?" - and the engine does the doing, through the same validators the human's taps go through. There is no privileged AI path into the rules, so an AI mistake is a *rejection* the driver reports rather than a corrupt board. It also makes self-play a millisecond-scale loop instead of a timer chain, which is what M12's differential harness will need. | 2026-08-21 |
| **D14** | Where do the AI's difficulty knobs live - `RulesOptions` or their own record? | **`AiTuning`, separate.** `RulesOptions` is the PARITY register: every flag there is a JS bug we reproduce, and the ship gate is that it is back to all-defaults with the flags deleted. Difficulty knobs are permanent design surface. Putting them in the same struct would make "zero active flags" unreachable forever. `AiTuning.JsDefault` reproduces the browser AI exactly; the parity flags it consults (`AiTakesGuaranteedKillFirst`, `AbsorberIsWeakestBlocker`, `AiRazeUsesHeuristic`) stay in RulesOptions where they belong. | 2026-08-21 |
| **D15** | The JS AI harvests before drawing, never enters the phase machine, and fuels its face-downs on leftover vault mana *before* harvesting. | **The AI runs the real phase machine** (already decided at M6): Upkeep → Draw → Action → End, same as the player. The only consequence is that the fuel step now runs after the harvest rather than before, which would be a real difference - except the JS AI never sets a card face-down, so it never owns a charge to fuel. The step is ported, unreachable, and commented as such. | 2026-08-21 |
| **D16** | PORT_PLAN M12 deliverable 3 says "patch `Math.random` onto the same PCG32 stream in the JS" so both engines deal the same cards. It cannot work. | **Do not make the harness depend on RNG agreement at all.** C#'s `NextInt(bound)` REJECTION-samples to kill modulo bias; the JS does `Math.floor(Math.random()*bound)`, one draw, scaled. Fed the identical stream they consume it differently the moment a rejection fires, and every later draw desyncs — so identical decks are unreachable by construction, and chasing it would mean either shipping the JS's modulo bias or testing an RNG path we do not ship. Instead the harness INJECTS both decks (`MatchSetup.NewMatch` already has the overload) and replays a recorded command trace; the AI's random choices are already baked into that trace as explicit DeclareAttack commands. The harness then tests the RULES, which is its actual job, and RNG parity becomes a separate, far smaller question for M16. | 2026-08-21 |
| **D17** | The JS has no owner-generic action layer - `doHarvest`, `place` and `castSpell` are hardcoded to 'you' and the AI's equivalents are inlined in `foeTurn`. Which path does the harness replay? | **The PLAYER path, re-expressed owner-generically out of the JS's own helpers.** The rules being executed stay the JS's rules; only their addressing changes. The player path is canonical because the port is symmetric and the player path is the fuller implementation - the AI's inline copies skip gates the player's honour. Every such choice is marked CANON in replay.mjs. Two real asymmetries had to be bridged explicitly: `startTurn` draws and fixes deficits only on the foe branch (and never sets it a phase at all), and the wall's virtual row index is -1 for a player attack but ROWS.length for the foe's. | 2026-08-21 |
| **D18** | Both engines let a creature MOVE after it has declared an attack — declaring taps it, and `moveSpent` only reads `moved`. What happens to the declaration? Found by the M12 fuzz tier; no scripted trace ever does it. | **It dies with the vacancy.** `live` (15_combat.js:312) rebuilds every attacker by reading the declaration's stored COORDINATE, so an attacker that walked away meets an empty cell and never strikes. `CombatResolver.LiveAttacker` is now the one predicate for "is this declaration still real", asked by BOTH the deferred-block collection and the main partition — a defender asked to block an attack that will never resolve spends its one block for the turn, which is a real difference, not a cosmetic one. The port does NOT inherit the other half of the JS behaviour: where the JS strikes with whatever creature moved into the cell behind the attacker, the port holds the unit id and strikes with nobody (spec 03 §17 risk 2, decided at M5). That single substitution case is the harness's ONE declared exception (`DECLARED_IDENTITY` in replay.mjs), counted and printed on every replay so it can never quietly widen. | 2026-08-21 |
| **D19** | The M12 comparison surface was a loose projection (sorted hand names, zone counts). Tighten it toward the state hash, or widen the projection? | **Widen the projection, and do not chase byte-identical hashing.** For the JS to reproduce `StateCodec`'s byte stream it would have to mirror every field and its order, and every later codec tweak would break the harness for reasons that have nothing to do with the rules. Instead the projection now carries every field EITHER engine mutates: the per-turn flags (`moved`/`moved2`/`paid`/`blocked`), the transient Overcharge discharge, the upkeep-paid ledger, per-unit colour/cost/upkeep/keyword numbers, and hand, deck and graveyard as ORDERED lists rather than counts — a hand index in a command is only meaningful against an order, and grave order is the order things died in, which is the observable half of combat sequencing. Tightening it immediately paid for itself: structure grave records were being written under their bid (`vault`) instead of their card name (`Mana Vault`). | 2026-08-21 |

## Session decisions — 2026-08-22 (M13 slice 1)

| # | Question | Answer | Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D20** | Design 02 §5.1 says author the card frame as `CardFrame.uxml` + `card.uss` and bake it. Follow that? | **Author it in C# as a `VisualElement` subclass instead; keep everything else about the decision.** Three reasons, in order of weight: (1) USS has no gradient functions, and this frame IS gradients - an ivory plate, a radial cost circle, a lozenge sweep, an art vignette - so the choice was never "markup vs code", it was "generated ramps vs shipped PNGs an artist must maintain"; (2) every element-tinted value has to be written from C# at bind time anyway, because USS custom properties cannot be set per instance at runtime; (3) this project is built headlessly from a shell, and UXML/USS authoring assumes the UI Builder. The four scales are four `Bind(model, palette, width)` calls. | 2026-08-22 |
| **D21** | The 76 non-ASCII glyphs (GAPS P0, "tofu risk"). Which fonts, and how is coverage guaranteed? | **Cinzel + EB Garamond (the reference build's own faces, both OFL) as dynamic assets, with Noto Symbols 2 / Symbols / Math / Emoji / Sans JP as STATIC baked fallbacks - and a generated glyph list gating the lot.** `tools/export_glyphs.mjs` scans the reference source and card data for every non-ASCII character; the pipeline routes each to the first font that measurably HAS it (block-guessing lost ⚔, ⚒, ⚙ and every arrow on the first attempt) and bakes only those glyphs; `FontCoverageTests` fails the build if any character has no home. Static fallbacks are stripped of their source TTF, which keeps a 5.3 MB kanji face out of the player build for the sake of eleven characters. | 2026-08-22 |
| **D22** | How is a presentation change verified, when no test can assert "it looks right"? | **`tools/view-probe.sh` renders a real UI Toolkit panel to a PNG from batchmode.** A build-and-deploy round trip is minutes and needs a browser; this is seconds and produces an image that can be looked at directly. It must run WITHOUT `-nographics` (no graphics device, no repaint) and drives the panel's repaint entry point by reflection, because a runtime panel otherwise only paints from the player loop - which `-executeMethod` blocks before ever ticking. That is exactly why the first probe wrote a blank white image. | 2026-08-22 |

## Session decisions — 2026-08-24 (M13 slice 4: the numbers, the tiles, the clash)

| # | Question | Answer | Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D23** | "Attack and HP should be reduced by 10 — 3000 ATK becomes 300." Rescale the CARD DATA, or only what is printed? | **Only what is printed.** The registry's ×500 stat scale and the 10000-point life pool are what the M12 differential harness pins ply-for-ply against the living JS; moving them would invalidate the goldens, the fuzz traces and the V4 validator for a change nobody can see in the rules. `StatScale.Show` (pure, in Rules, because the rules text producers print statlines into sentences) divides by ten, and `View.Stat` wraps it with the glyphs. Nothing the engine computes moves by a point. What this actually fixed was worse than the scale itself: the display had TWO scales — the board overlays and the deck builder divided by 500, so a 3000-attack dragon read as "6", while the card frames and the wall rails printed the raw number, so the same dragon read "3000" in the hand and the wall it was hitting read "10000". | 2026-08-24 |
| **D24** | The card lying on a tile covered under half of it. What is a plate's footprint? | **The tile's face, exactly — accepting a 4% stretch.** The old constant said `0.98` of a cell "along the card's long axis", which sounds like a full tile and is not one: the card was then sized DOWN from that by its own aspect, so a 0.72 × 0.98 card sat on a 1.00 × 1.45 tile. A tile is 1.45× deeper than it is wide and a card is 1.39× taller than it is wide, so filling the tile costs the card about 4% of stretch along its length — invisible beside the sin(42°) foreshortening the whole plate is already under, and cheap for a board with no gaps in it. | 2026-08-24 |
| **D25** | "Buildings are floating far too high above their tiles." Where do a standee's feet go? | **At the FRONT of its own tile (11% of the tile's depth up from the near edge, 12% for a structure — the reference stylesheet's `.spritebob { bottom: 11% }`), not at the cell centre.** The centre is geometrically where the cell is and visually where nothing is: now that the card covers the whole tile, its near half sits BELOW the point the figure stood on, and a building with half a card showing underneath it is a building hovering over one. Two smaller faults went with it — the shadow stayed at the cell centre while the figure moved (a shadow half a tile behind a figure reads as flight), and the sprite was lifted by its BUDGETED height rather than its rendered one, so any cut-out clamped by the width cap floated by the difference. | 2026-08-24 |
| **D26** | "The campaign globe is hollow." What was hollow about it? | **Nothing was missing: every tile fan was wound INWARD.** `Cull Back` therefore removed the near hemisphere, and what looked like a globe was the inside of the far one — lit, coloured, plausible, and see-through wherever two tiles did not overlap. It survived a milestone because a shell with the lights on looks like a globe until you put something underneath it: adding a crust made it obvious instantly, because the crust drew over the plates it was supposed to lie beneath. Fixed by flipping the plate fans, the crust fans and the plate side walls (found by the test, not the eye — a side wall's normal is tangential, so the naive radial dot product reads zero for it and its sign is noise). `PresentationTests.EveryGlobeTriangleFacesOutOfTheSphere` now checks all 3840 triangles in both senses. | 2026-08-24 |
| **D27** | The globe's shader has no `LightMode` tag and no depth pass, so URP never primes its depth — the globe has always been drawn back-to-front by `Cull Back` and index order rather than depth-sorted. Fix the shader, the mesh order, or both? | **Both, and say so in the shader.** The pass declares `UniversalForward` and an explicit `ZTest LEqual`, and a `DepthOnly` pass is added so the globe is in the prepass and in the camera's depth texture instead of being a hole in both. But `GlobeView` still emits each tile BOTTOM-UP — crust, then side wall, then face — because a picture that is only correct while the depth buffer cooperates is a picture waiting to break, and the ordering costs nothing. The crust is part of the tile mesh for the same reason: two renderers sharing one material and one bounds centre do not reliably sort against each other, and a mesh cannot disagree with itself. | 2026-08-24 |
| **D28** | The battle cut-in has to draw two cards that no longer exist on the board — the loser is graved inside the same `Apply`. Where does it read them from? | **A one-frame-old SNAPSHOT of every unit, refreshed in LateUpdate after the frame's events are pumped, and kept for 12 s after a unit leaves the board.** Nothing that reads `GameState` can draw a fight, because by the time `AttackDeclared` is drained the fight is over. This also forced a real fix: `MatchController` pumped events once per frame at the top of `Update`, so a command applied anywhere else (the IMGUI rail, the AI driver) had a LateUpdate — and therefore a snapshot refresh — between it and its own events. Events are drained immediately after every command now, and the once-per-frame pump stays as a catch-all. | 2026-08-24 |
| **D29** | Combat v3 declares first and resolves later. Does the cut-in fire on the declaration or on the resolution? | **On the resolution, one per declaration, in declaration order.** A cut-in at declaration time is a picture of two cards that have not fought yet, and the fight itself then happens off screen with nothing but a log line. Each declaration is recorded as it is made (keyed by `DeclarationIndex`, which restarting at 0 is the only signal that a new combat has begun) and told when its damage lands — which can be seconds later, on the other side of a parked blocker choice. At most three per combat: an alpha strike of six declarations is one decision, not six, and nine seconds of cut-ins is a punishment for making it. | 2026-08-24 |
| **D30** | The unit stat overlays were IMGUI, floating 1.45 cells above each slot. Keep them there? | **Moved to UI Toolkit, hung off each tile's NEAR EDGE.** "I can't see the health of buildings I am attacking" was three faults at once: 1.45 cells above a slot is most of a row further up the screen under the tilt, so the foe's back row put its numbers behind the castle wall and the layer's own answer was to DROP them — the row you attack into was the row with no numbers; IMGUI draws with the built-in font, which has no ♥, ◆ or ⚔ and silently drops them (which is why the old overlay read "6 hp"); and a number is not a quantity, so there is a bar under the line now. It also gained a state the old one could not have: a unit your selected attacker may legally hit is marked as a TARGET, straight off BoardInput's engine-probed list. | 2026-08-24 |
| **D31** | Tapping ⚔ WALL selected the card underneath it. The three HUD panels publish their rects for BoardInput to refuse taps in; add a fourth? | **No — move the registration into the drawing.** Legacy Input cannot see IMGUI consume an event (Update even runs before the frame's GUI events), so every control over the field has to be published or it is invisible to the board. Publishing PANELS by hand worked because a panel is easy to remember; a loose button is not, and the attack row's buttons sit straight on the board. `MatchHud.Btn` registers the rect it is about to draw into `HudLayout.Control`, so a control that is drawn is blocked and the two cannot drift apart. Controls inside a scroll view stay on plain `GUI.Button`: their rects are in the view's own space, and the panel that owns the view already publishes itself. | 2026-08-24 |
| **D32** | A face-down card's invested mana: whose information is it? | **Both players'.** It was drawn for the owner only, which is the instinct you have about a face-down card and is wrong here: setting costs ◆1, you may pour in as much more as you like, and a card that will not have its cost when it is turned over simply fails (`Traps.ProvokeFaceDown` destroys an underfunded charge outright). Over-paying is therefore a BLUFF, and a bluff nobody can read is not one. A set trap consumed its ◆1 rather than banking it, so it reports that ◆1 — which is the point: a face-down showing ◆1 is either a trap or a creature nobody has funded, and telling those apart is the guess the mechanic is made of. | 2026-08-24 |
| **D33** | Structures in the foe's back row stood a third taller than the tile they were on. What is a standee's size measured against? | **Its own tile, AS IT PROJECTS — and the figure's own axes, not the world's.** Two separate errors. A billboard's screen height falls off as 1/z while its tile's screen height falls off faster (the tile is lying down, and its near edge is nearer than its far one), so a figure sized in world units grows relative to its ground the further away it stands: 1.64× the tile at the back row against 0.76× at the front. That is what the reference stylesheet's `cqh`/`cqw` units always meant — the cell as rendered — so the sizes are measured on screen now and converted back. The second error was in the conversion: a standee is a camera-facing billboard, so it grows along the CAMERA's up axis, and measuring against world +Y understates it by cos(42°) — every figure came out half again too big to make up the difference. | 2026-08-24 |

## Session decisions — 2026-08-29 (M13 slice 6: the numbers on the card, the attack group, the clash)

| # | Question | Answer | Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D34** | "The black bars under the card should display health — a meter with a number in it — and above the black bar, the Attack, Worker Amount and Base HP." The plate frame was built on the opposite principle: it carries the card's SHAPE, and the numbers live in a chip hanging off the tile. Which wins? | **The card.** The frame was drawing a stat bar with no stats in it and three ruled lines standing in for text; both are the thing they stood in for now — the stats band is a health METER with the number printed across it, and the ability box is a plaque reading attack / worker draw or upkeep / printed health. The frame's own argument against this ("no text is legible in a band twelve pixels tall") was answered by measuring the other axis: the band is the WIDEST thing on the card, and a number laid along it has room. The vitals chip is not deleted but demoted to a NAMEPLATE — name, the two keyword states that change, and the target ring — because printing the same health twice a finger's width apart is not redundancy, it is two things to check. | 2026-08-29 |
| **D35** | The foe's cards: face the one reader (Master Duel), or turn them round (a table)? | **Turn them round.** Each side's health meter then lands on its own edge of the board — yours along the near edge of your tiles, theirs along the far edge of theirs — so the two never sit in the same place, and a glance down the board is never reading someone else's numbers. Every READOUT is counter-rotated inside the turned plate (`UprightOnFoeCard`), because a card can be upside down and a number cannot; the meter's FILL is the one thing that does not turn, since a bar has no up and two boards draining in opposite directions is a thing to decode rather than read. It is a rotation, not a reflection — pinned by a handedness test, because a mirrored plate and a rotated one differ by a determinant and by nothing you can see on a symmetrical frame. | 2026-08-29 |
| **D36** | How are the numbers drawn, on a world-space sprite with no access to the gated font chain? | **Rastered, with the existing 3×5 bitmap font stretched about 1 : 1.4, at twice the band's own resolution — and sorted OVER the standee.** Three things fell out of measuring rather than guessing: the strip is limited by the card's WIDTH and has height going spare, so square cells throw away a third of the legible size; the cell size is an INTEGER, and at the band's own 192 texels the step from a size that fits to one that does not threw away a fifth of the width again; and the figure stands at the FRONT of its tile, so its shins cross both bands the numbers live in — a number behind a cut-out is not a number. The meter itself is quads rather than a raster (one texture per (hp, max) pair is a cache that grows with the fight); only the NUMBER is rastered, keyed by value. The attack mark is an X: an upright sword drawn in a dozen pixels is a blade three of them wide. | 2026-08-29 |
| **D37** | "When attacking with multiple monsters, I shouldn't have to individually select their target to be the same to attack together." | **A declared target opens an ASSAULT, and tapping your other ready creatures joins it.** Nothing in the rules changed: a joint attack has always been N declarations sharing a target, regrouped by target identity at resolve time (spec 03 §6.2), and there is deliberately no group command. What changed is that re-picking a target you have already picked is not a decision. Every player declaration now goes through one funnel (`MatchController.Declare`), so the wall and worker-stack buttons open a group exactly as a board tap does; the group dies with its declarations, with your action phase, or on ✕ CANCEL (**amended by D49** — the button was LATER, and it dropped only the aim), so a tap on your own creature means "select" again the moment the attack is over. The joiners wear a gold ring on their nameplate rather than a lit cell, because a lit cell is UNDER the card that covers the whole tile. | 2026-08-29 |
| **D38** | A declaration parks a BlockerRequest on the defender, which the AI answers on its own 0.35 s beat — during which every command, the next declaration included, is rejected as ChoicePending. Queue the joins, or answer the choice? | **Answer it, immediately, in the declaration path only.** That window is invisible while a declaration is the END of an interaction and fatal once it is the middle of one: tapping three creatures into one attack puts two of the taps inside it. The defender's answer is part of resolving YOUR command, so it does not wait for a beat. Deliberately narrow — blocker requests parked on the foe, nothing else — so the cut-in's presentation hold is untouched and a choice parked mid-RESOLUTION still runs on the beat, where the theatre can pace it. | 2026-08-29 |
| **D39** | "When combat occurs, the cards involved should be larger... when multi-combat happens, the cards can be stacked." | **A cut-in card is about three tenths of the screen wide, or as much as its height allows — which on any landscape screen is what decides — and a joint attack is ONE cut-in with the attackers fanned.** The old cap was 132 px, a pair of postage stamps in the middle of a board the cut-in exists to interrupt; every font size inside the card was clamped too, so scaling it alone would have produced a poster with eight-point type on it. ## Session decisions — 2026-08-30 (M17: password-linked multiplayer)

| # | Question | Answer | Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D40** | Host-authoritative snapshots (what the JS shipped) or deterministic command lockstep? | **Lockstep. The wire carries commands, never state.** The JS broadcast a full 25-40 KB snapshot after every change because its engine was not deterministic and its state was not reconstructible — the snapshot *was* the desync heal. M12 removed that need: 10,000 random legal commands across 25 matches replay with zero divergence, and `StateCodec.Hash` collapses a match to 64 bits. Measured result: a whole 200-ply match is **200 messages and 13.8 KB**, against 25-40 KB *per change*. Three things fall out that are worth more than the bandwidth — a desync cannot be silent (every frame carries the sender's hash before applying), a reconnect is a replay, and a match becomes seed + decks + command log, which is exactly the artifact `tools/diffjs/` already eats. | 2026-08-30 |
| **D41** | Who orders the two peers' commands? | **Nobody — the rules already do, and where they nearly don't, the netcode does.** A parked `PendingRequest` admits only its `Responder`, and every other handler gates on phase and turn, so two peers cannot normally both have a legal command; each may therefore apply its own move instantly and publish it, with no host round trip. The exception is real: `GameState.IsInteractive` reads like the guarantee and **has no callers**, and `SendBankedManaHandler` has no phase gate — so at `Phase == End` the outgoing side may still move a bank while the incoming side begins its turn. Two legal, non-commuting commands. The M12 fuzz corpus has 218 of them, all `sendMana`. `NetSession.LocalGate` refuses everything but the incoming side's own `BeginTurn` at End. | 2026-08-30 |
| **D42** | Fix that hole in `SendBankedManaHandler` instead? | **Not in this milestone.** A phase gate there is probably the right rules change — every other handler has one — but it changes the game and invalidates 218 plies across 24 of 25 golden traces, so it is a rules decision with a corpus regeneration attached. That belongs to M16, where the parity register is being emptied anyway, not to a netcode milestone that can close the hole locally and leave solo play untouched. **M16 checklist item: add the gate, re-cut the goldens, delete `LocalGate`'s phase clause.** | 2026-08-30 |
| **D43** | The guest's engine has them as `Side.Foe`. Mirror the wire so both peers think they are `Side.You`, as the JS did? | **No — the VIEW takes a seat.** Mirroring is not merely unattractive here, it is unavailable: `MatchSetup.NewMatch` draws You's deck before Foe's off one shared RNG stream, so two mirrored setups diverge on the first shuffle, and determinism is the whole thing being protected. So `Seat.Local` / `Seat.Remote` replace the ~90 view sites that read `Side.You` to mean "me", and the camera gains 180° of yaw for the far seat. Two sites are geometry, not identity, and were left alone or fixed by hand: the flat rotation a card plate lies at (`FlatOnTile` names a world basis, and reads correctly from the opposite camera precisely because it is absolute), and `BoardView.RowMaterial`, which keys the warm/cold ground wash off `RowKey` and so contains no `Side.You` for a substitution sweep to find. | 2026-08-30 |
| **D44** | ntfy.sh over HTTP (one code path everywhere, WebGL included) or MQTT over WebSocket? | **MQTT, to three public brokers at once.** The ntfy design was written, and then measured: its free tier allows a 60-request burst refilled at one per five seconds and 250 publishes a day, so a 350 ms poll drains the burst in twenty seconds and a 5 s keepalive alone exhausts the day inside twenty-one minutes. Then ntfy.sh **stopped answering this machine entirely** after a few dozen probe requests, with every other host reachable. A relay that playing the game can get you cut off from is not a transport. A broker connection costs one socket and has no per-message budget; publishing to all three and reading all three means the pair meets if any one broker works for both, with no negotiation — and the duplicate copies were free, because the protocol had to tolerate duplicates anyway. | 2026-08-30 |
| **D45** | Reconnection: read it back out of the relay's cache, or out of the other peer? | **Out of the other peer.** The cache answer was written first and was tied to ntfy's 12-hour retention; a broker retains nothing, so the peers keep the log themselves — fifteen bytes a frame, six kilobytes for a long match. `Hello` and `Join` are then the same message in opposite directions, and whichever end still holds a live match answers an introduction by sending `Start` plus every frame since. It works in both directions with no extra message kind, it needs no relay to remember anything, and it deleted the whole cold-scan phase along with its "is this cached game still live" heuristics. | 2026-08-30 |
| **D46** | Hidden information: both peers hold the whole state, opponent's hand included. Acceptable? | **Yes, and documented, as the JS did.** It is the friend-play model: the attack requires a cheater who already has your password, and the four properties lockstep buys are worth more to a game whose multiplayer exists so two people who know each other can play and so balance data can be generated. The upgrade path is host-authoritative redaction, and it can be built later without touching the transport, the crypto, the lobby or the view — which is why those four are what this milestone actually built. | 2026-08-30 |
| **D47** | Can the scripted AI play over the network? | **No, and it is worth knowing why.** `AiChoices` rolls its face-down and structure decisions off the MATCH RNG, so running the policy on one peer advances that peer's stream and not the other's — an instant desync. Human duels contain no AI, so this costs the feature nothing, but "put the AI on the other end" is not free and neither is a spectator that runs one. It is also why the netcode tests drive `FuzzPolicy` (own `Pcg32`, touches nothing) rather than the AI. | 2026-08-30 |
