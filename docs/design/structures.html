Spawn Row Duel Structures

Every structure you can raise, what it actually does in the rules engine, and what it’s worth per turn. Ability text here is read from the C# handlers — not from the card’s flavour string. Numbers reflect the 7 September passes: forges are pure mana, the Base is one of each, the Outpost stands on the middle row, and the Cannon Tower fires for half with a Bombard tier above it.

Opening position

The board starts empty. You have ◆0, your commander’s 2–3 back-row workers (not summoning-sick, so turn 1 harvests), and 4 cards. Turn 1 you harvest ◆2 — and the Base is the only thing you can both afford and unlock.

What unlocks what

Forges

One variant per element

Nine named variants — Emberforge, Tidewell, Stonewell, Galewell, Thornwell, Stormforge, Dawnwell, Gloomwell, Empyreum — and the element is decoration. Mana is one generic pool, so a Tidewell and an Emberforge are the same card with different art. A forge’s colour only matters in that it rides down its own upgrade chain.

Vaults are the only structures at ⚒0 — they take no workers and give none. Banked mana is also the only mana that exists before you harvest, so it is what pays an upkeep shortfall. Raze an opponent’s vault before their end of turn and you destroy the mana it would have kept, not just the building.

Cannon Tower

Menu & upgrade
◆4♥400⚒−1

At your harvest — after Move, Pay and Sacrifice, before the workers dig — deals ⚔50 to one enemy creature, if its row can crew it: a tower in a row whose shortfall is still unsettled stays silent. It does not pick the nearest — it scans their front row left to right, then the centre, then their back row, and hits the first non-worker creature it finds. Never structures, never workers, never the life pool. Built this turn, it fires at your next harvest, so it has to survive a full enemy turn first.

Needs a Forge · takes ⚒1 from its row

Menu

What each one is really worth

Per-turn yield

The printed ◆ yield is only half the income. Worker pools are derived from the sum of ⚒ support in each row, and every ready worker harvests ◆1. So a structure’s true output is support + printed yield — and the economy pass made that the design: at the same price a Forge is all mana, an Encampment is all workers, and the Base is one of each.

Assumes the row’s worker figure stays non-negative and its workers are untapped. Workers arrive summoning-sick, so a structure raised this turn first harvests next turn. The two towers are the only structures with negative support — a Cannon Tower takes one worker out of its row for as long as it stands, a Bombard two.

Rules that apply to all of them

Engine behaviour

Never played from hand

Structures are not deck cards. They are raised from the commander’s BUILD menu during the Action phase, paid in generic ◆. Every commander’s menu holds the same ten slots — twelve for a dual.

Where they can stand

Twenty-one of the thirty-five cells: your back row, your front row, and the whole contested centre row. Never on enemy ground. The cell must be empty, and a tier’s row gate now applies to the build as well as the upgrade — Longhouse front, Outpost middle.

Upgrading is in place

Same tile, same object, same banked ◆. It costs the full price of the new tier, carries damage through, repairs nothing, and does not re-check prerequisites — razing your Base will not stop a Keep becoming a Citadel.

They never fight

Structures never move, attack, block or retaliate. They are always legal attack targets and take damage without hitting back. Nothing heals or repairs a structure anywhere in the codebase.

Menu order is AI priority

The scripted opponent builds down the list in order — Base, Forge, Encampment, Longhouse, Vault, Outpost, Bulwark, Cannon Tower, Reliquary, Grand Forge — and takes the first listed upgrade target, so its Outposts always become Towers and never Bastions.

Effects fire before anything else

Mana and the Reliquary resolve at the very start of your turn, scanning your front row, back row, then centre, columns left to right. Towers fire later, at your harvest, once the upkeep is settled and only from a row that can crew them. Kills wait for the sweep immediately after.

Under review

Data vs. engine

Grand Forge is in every build menu

Balance

The build gate checks menu membership, not the buildable flag — and all 36 commanders list grandforge:<element> last. Once you own any Forge you can spend ◆6 to raise a second Grand Forge on a fresh cell and keep the Forge, which strictly beats upgrading for the same ◆6. No cap on how many. The AI is written around it and only limits itself to one per colour.

Placement.IsInBuildList — nothing in the rules engine reads StructureDef.Buildable

The Wall effect does nothing

Dead code

Bulwark and Bastion carry StructEffect.Wall, and no line in the rules engine branches on it — only two view files that print flavour. They are big bodies with support and nothing more. The inspector claims a Bulwark “can intercept and be raided”; interception collects creatures and workers only.

CardEnums.cs:52 declares it · the only other hits are the string parser and CardTextService

The Villager effect is a no-op

Dead code

It adds structValue to the row’s worker figure — and Longhouse and Barracks both ship structValue: 0. All their workforce comes from plain support, exactly like the Encampment’s. The inspector says a Longhouse “trains a free Minion (0/100 ⚒) into its owner’s base pool each turn”; that code does not exist.

WorkerMath.cs:34 is the only consumer · text at CardTextService.cs:119

Cannon Tower is both a build and an upgrade

Asymmetry

It sits in every menu at ◆4 and is an Outpost upgrade target, but its own upgradedFrom is empty. So the Outpost route costs ◆6 for the same building, and a Cannon Tower does not count as an Outpost for prerequisite purposes. The catalog validator flags this and names the Tower as the known offender — as a warning only. The Bombard tier above it is upgrade-only and properly linked.

CatalogValidator · upgrade-graph symmetry check V8, warn-only

Back-row Encampments are a dead end

Trap

Encampment has no row gate, but Longhouse is front-row-only and the upgrade handler re-checks the target tier’s gate. An Encampment raised in the back row can never be grown, and nothing in the UI warns you before you spend the ◆2.

StructureHandlers.cs:89–90 · rejected as WrongRowForTier
