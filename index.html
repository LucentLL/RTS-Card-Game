How to Play
The Ember Bastion
Your back row IS your stronghold. An undefended strike into any open back-row column drains your ♥ life pool — when it empties, you lose.

Win — breach the back row. Your element choice sets your life pool (♥) and your base workers; element is otherwise a synergy attribute (Yu-Gi-Oh style), not a mana cost. There is no command-center card: attacks aimed at an open column of the enemy back row that go unintercepted deal their full ⚔ to the defender's life. Bodies standing in the back row must be fought instead.

The board. Five rows: each side's back row is its own soil — the enemy may never enter yours, only attack it. The three middle rows (both fronts + the center) are contested: either army may advance into them, marching one row per move. New cards deploy only in your back or front row (structures may also be built on the center's flanking slots). Movement is one square in any direction — sideways, forward, back, or diagonal. Targeting is by row distance: adjacent strikes land directly; deeper strikes can be intercepted by defenders in any row they cross.

Building (RTS). Structures are not in your deck — your commander builds them. Tap your command center or the ⚒ Build button to open the menu, then tap an open space to raise it. Building costs mana and follows a tech tree: a Foundry first, which unlocks the element Forges (each forge yields ◆ every turn) and the Longhouse; a forge in turn unlocks the Bulwark and Grand Forges. Build as often as you can afford it.

Turn phases. Each turn runs through set phases, shown in the tracker by the End Turn button. Upkeep — settle your workforce (move, sacrifice, or pay), then press ⛏ Harvest to collect mana. Draw — click your deck to draw a card. Action — summon, move, build, and attack freely. Combat is a sub-phase of Action: it opens each time you declare an attacker and can repeat with different attackers. End — end-of-turn effects resolve, then the turn passes to your opponent.

Workers & the upkeep. Each row's worker number is the running total of the worker stats of the cards in it — structures add (a Barracks might be ⚒+3), monsters subtract their upkeep (a Dragon ⚒−2). At Upkeep you may reposition creatures (this uses their move for the turn; a second forced move also taps them), then press ⛏ Harvest — every settled worker extracts automatically (◆1 each, the same in every row) straight into your mana pool. A row gone negative (or an army camped in the enemy front, which nothing supports) must be settled first, creature by creature: ⤧ Move it back, ◆ Pay its keep, or ✖ Sacrifice it — ⛏ Harvest stays locked until every row is balanced. Your left tower lists each row's worker count, stacked to match the board.

Mana. Harvesting yields generic ◆ — one pool, no colors. Every card costs only its ◆ number, whatever its element. Element (Fire, Water, Forest…) is a synergy attribute only, like a Yu-Gi-Oh attribute: it drives card theming and elemental keyword effects, not what mana you need. Mana persists between turns — it keeps building until you spend it (or spend it keeping over-extended lines fed).

Face-downs & banking. Setting a card costs ◆1, placed onto the card (it counts toward funding — no free hand-dumping). Pour more mana into it across turns; when fully funded it flips (surplus banks on the card, visible to all). A face-down attacked mid-funding is interrupted and destroyed with its invested mana. Face-down a full turn? It flips with haste.

Attack — alone or as a group. Tap ready creatures (same row to group) — or on PC, drag a selection box over them like an RTS — then tap an enemy unit, structure, face-down, worker stack, or the command center itself. The defender answers a traveling strike with interceptors from any row it crosses. Combat is simultaneous (First Strike aside) with no spillover.

Note: a prototype. Open questions, deliberately parked: the exact colored-cost rule (currently 1 pip + any remainder), storage structures that convert colors, a Ranged keyword, and the soldier-production model.
